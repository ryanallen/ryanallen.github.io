The Voynich text measured the same way as the languages a writer in the Alps of the early 1400s could have known: Latin, German, northern Italian, French, Czech, Hebrew, Greek and Arabic, each its nearest free text of the period, plain and with its words abbreviated, its vowels taken out or written as a syllabary. Beside them stand a cipher made to look like the Voynich and texts generated from rules. Each bar is a text's distance from the Voynich on four groups of numbers: its letters, its words, the order of its words, and how predictable it is to a small language model. Shorter is nearer; the bars start a little short of the nearest, so the gaps show. Counted on the 187 development pages.
