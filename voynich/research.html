No one can read the Voynich manuscript yet. This illustrated book, made in the early 1400s and kept at Yale, is full of plants, stars, people, and unknown writing. Here are the pictures, the text, and the tests of proposed solutions. Each page shows what the evidence supports and where it stops. Some pages are saved for a final test.

The pictures

PlantsSee each plant in the book, how confidently it can be named, and who has proposed a name. Plant comparisonsCompare seven Voynich plants with a 1400s Italian herbal. See what looks alike and what does not. DrawingsSee the people, animals, stars, buildings, and objects. Each picture has a proposed name and a confidence level. Zodiac and bathing pagesCount the figures around each zodiac sign and look at the bathing pools. The pages may follow degrees of the zodiac, not days of the month.

The words

SearchFind a word or letter sequence and see where it appears on the manuscript page. LabelsLook at words beside pictures. So far, repeated words do not show that any label names its picture. ScribesThe ink supports two groups of scribes. See how the page groups compare with earlier counts of two, five, or more. Text readerSee a program turn page photographs into letters. Twenty unseen pages show what it gets right and wrong. Letter patternsSee which letters appear at the start or end of words, and which ones behave alike. No sound is assigned to them.

Readings tested

Language comparisonsCompare the text with eight languages, a cipher, and generated text. No tested language comes close. Proposed solutionsSee what each proposed solution predicts, how it was tested, and where it fails or remains untested.
The letter counts come from tracings of the manuscript. Trace a few letters, and leave your name to be credited when the glyph map, the OCR and the font are released.
