An open study of the Voynich manuscript (Yale, Beinecke MS 408), the illustrated book of the early 1400s written in a script no one has read. Every page here shows its evidence beside the manuscript's own images, says how sure each finding is, and is built from files and tests anyone can check. Pages held back as a final test stay closed until a reading is ready for them.

The pictures

PlantsEvery plant drawn on the herbal and pharmaceutical pages, grouped by how surely it can be named (secure, likely, possible, unknown), with every published identification and who made it. Herbal likenessesVoynich plants set beside the drawings of Penn's Erbario, a northern Italian herbal of the 1400s: what agrees, what does not, and the Erbario's own name and note in English. DrawingsEvery drawing that is not a plant: zodiac signs, creatures, the castle, suns, moons and stars, wheels, women, jars and pools, grouped by what they show and how surely each can be named. Zodiac and bathing pagesThe twelve zodiac rings with each woman, her star, her tub and her label in order, and the bathing section's pools grouped by the color of their water.

The words

SearchFind any Voynich word or part of a word across every page, with each place it stands shown on the page image. LabelsThe single words written beside stars, zodiac women, jars, roots and leaves, each with the thing it labels, and the tests of whether a label names what it stands beside. HandsHow many scribes wrote it, from the ink of every letter: the pages grouped with no hand named, every proposed count set beside the groups, and the later hands of the folio numbers, month names and f116v. ReaderA program that reads the Voynich script from a page photograph into EVA, tested on twenty pages it never saw: each line beside the scan and the transcription, every wrong letter marked, and its error rate by scribe. Sign gridThe Voynich signs grouped by how they behave, as Kober and Ventris grouped the signs of Linear B before anyone could read it, with no sound values assumed.

Readings tested

Language fingerprintsThe Voynich text measured the same way as the languages an Alpine writer of the early 1400s could have known (Latin, German, northern Italian, French, Czech, Hebrew, Greek, Arabic), beside ciphers and made-up text. Proposed readingsEvery proposed decipherment and theory put to one test, written down before it is run: what it claims, how it was run, its scorecard against the manuscript's own behavior, and its verdict.
The letters and words these pages count come from people tracing the manuscript sign by sign. Trace a few letters, and leave your name to be credited when the glyph map, the OCR and the font are released.
