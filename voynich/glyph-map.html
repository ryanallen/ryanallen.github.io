The glyph map ties every traced word and letter of the Voynich manuscript to the exact pixels it covers in Yale's photographs. Most Voynich statistics are counted off a typed transcription. This lets you count, cluster and test on the ink itself. Take it all, or one page at a time.

What's in it

words.tsv has one row per word: its ID in the Zandbergen–Landini transliteration (ZL 3b), its reading in EVA (the standard alphabet for typing Voynich letters), trust, word box and the box round its traced ink. letters.tsv has one row per letter, with its box and center. masks/<page>.npz holds every traced pixel, with its word and letter. pages.tsv links each of Yale's images. Coordinates are pixels of Yale's full-size JPEGs. No images are included; fetch them from Yale. The README in each zip gives every field and a short Python example.
