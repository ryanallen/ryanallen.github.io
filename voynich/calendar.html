Twelve pages of the Voynich manuscript ring each zodiac sign with small women, each holding a star with a word beside her, and some standing in tubs. Here every woman is shown in her ring, in order clockwise from the top, with her tub and her label (click a label to find the word everywhere). Below: the bathing section's pools grouped by the color of their water, and each sign set beside three German zodiacs of the 1400s. Pages marked final are held out for the last test.
What was found
  • One woman per degree. Every sign has 30 women (Aries and Taurus as two pages of 15; Pisces 29), months of 31 days included: the pages count degrees of a sign, not days.
  • Tubs only in March, April and May (Pisces, Aries, Taurus), the months the bathing calendars of the 1400s set for bathing, with herb baths in May.
  • The labels do not repeat at the same degree from sign to sign, as a calendar's would.
  • Green water is the rule in the bathing pools (18 of 24), with no pattern by page.
  • The nearest zodiac: St. Gallen Cod. Sang. 827 (Lake Constance, 1425–1428) alone shares the crossbowman Sagittarius and the lizard-like Scorpio.
